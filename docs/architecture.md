# Architecture

## Current structure

- `src/main.ts` is the browser composition root. It creates the scene bundle, island, world registry, reusable particle/shake FX, all three powers, the power tray, and targeting controller; scatters the initial settlement; spawns six villagers; registers the floating island; runs seeded fire spread, grave aging, villager behavior, and population checks on the fixed simulation step; advances power timelines, cooldown presentation, particles, shake, ponds, capped fire-light selection, and the shared 12fps visual clock alongside display-rate composer rendering; and dismisses the splash after the first frame.
- `src/scene.ts` owns renderer creation and WebGL fallback, golden-hour lighting and VSM shadows, the post-processing composer, the responsive perspective camera and constrained orbit controls, the procedural gradient sky, six cloud clusters, reduced-motion-aware ambient movement, and the eight-second auto-rotate pause after camera interaction.
- `src/core/motion.ts` exposes a live reduced-motion media-query check without importing Three.js.
- `src/core/rng.ts` provides the seedable pseudo-random source used for reproducible simulation and procedural generation.
- `src/core/noise.ts` builds deterministic 2D value noise and fractal Brownian motion from integer lattice hashing.
- `src/core/events.ts` defines the shared game-event contract and a synchronous subscription bus that safely tolerates unsubscription during dispatch.
- `src/core/loop.ts` separates fixed-step simulation from display-rate rendering and provides the 12fps quantized animation clock. Its lifecycle is idempotent: duplicate starts cannot create parallel RAF chains, and stopping from either simulation or rendering prevents any further work from being scheduled.
- `src/core/timeline.ts` schedules one-shot callbacks for the staged anticipation, impact, and aftermath beats used by powers. Events execute in timestamp order even when a caller registers them out of order.
- `src/fx/clayMaterial.ts` creates the shared toon material, including a cached soft three-band ramp and shader patches for fingerprint-scale normal breakup and hand-mixed color variation. Flat variants skip shader noise and use a distinct program-cache key.
- `src/fx/decals.ts` provides the pure numeric helpers for radial scorch accumulation and base-to-decal channel blending, keeping vertex-color painting independent of Three.js.
- `src/fx/wobble.ts` mutates a geometry once by displacing vertices along their normals with seeded, decorrelated low-frequency noise, then rebuilds its normals.
- `src/fx/emotes.ts` owns one canvas-generated six-cell glyph atlas and the billboarded `EmoteSprite`. Each sprite selects an atlas cell and applies its pop, bob, and lifetime only on quantized frames.
- `src/fx/particles.ts` owns the fixed-capacity `ParticleBurst` pool: one wobbled icosahedron geometry, one clay material, and one `InstancedMesh` whose dead slots are hidden at scale zero and reused without allocation. Per-slot position, velocity, rotation, spin, gravity, size, and lifetime live in typed arrays and advance at display rate.
- `src/fx/shake.ts` provides trauma-squared noise shake with previous-offset cleanup plus a reusable DOM flash overlay. Shake reads reduced-motion live and stops without leaving camera drift; flash substitutes a soft 30%-opacity fade for the normal bright impact flash.
- `src/world/craters.ts` is the pure crater geometry contract: it calculates accumulated bowl depth and resolves whether a point lies inside a crater rim.
- `src/world/fire.ts` is the pure wildfire state machine and sole fire-tuning source. `FireSystem` advances tree/hut burn timers, performs seeded one-step-per-tick spread, protects burnt records from re-ignition, keeps campfire sources alive until doused, and returns typed ignition/burn-out/douse events while mutating plain `Flammable` records.
- `src/world/island.ts` owns the procedural radial-grid grass surface, dirt fringe, rocky lathed underside, persistent crater deformation, accumulated scorch painting, and the crater-aware ground-height query used by all grounded entities. Crater and scorch stamps derive a bounded band of radial-grid indices instead of scanning every vertex.
- `src/world/scatter.ts` is the pure deterministic layout solver. It rejection-samples every new footprint against all earlier placements so trees, huts, the build site, and the central campfire cannot overlap.
- `src/world/objects.ts` defines the visual `Tree`, `Hut`, `Campfire`, `Pond`, `Grave`, and `FlowerPatch` entities from wobbled clay primitives. Trees and huts expose stable `Flammable` state and each owns one reusable 3–5-blob flame group, drifting smoke blobs, and a shadowless point light; their 12fps visual updates translate ignition, dousing, and burn-out into scorched crowns, stumps, or ruins. The campfire has a permanent scattered state with displaced stones/logs and cannot flare or re-ignite afterward. Ponds own a translucent quantized water surface and optional floating fish, graves age from fresh after 60 seconds, and each flower patch is one 36-instance bloom mesh constrained to the island footprint. Removed pond/flower resources and expired fruit dispose their geometries and materials. The module also owns instanced stones, normal shadow casting, and low-FX blob-shadow fallbacks.
- `src/world/world.ts` owns the small world registry and its linear spatial queries, target resolution order, shared event bus, villager alarms, pure-to-visual fire-event bridge, targeted ignition/dousing/destruction, nearest-camera three-fire-light cap, and delayed hut-based repopulation. It is also the persistent consequence boundary used by powers: it kills the nearest valid villager, empties ponds, scatters the campfire, creates and updates typed graves/ponds/flowers, removes blooms intersecting new craters, grows fruit, and re-grounds standing props after terrain deformation. Target resolution ignores dead villagers and the scattered campfire, and repeated rain near an existing bloom reuses that patch.
- `src/villagers/fsm.ts` is the pure theatrical behavior selector. It fixes the villager state union at exactly eight states, applies danger/mourning/rebuilding overrides in priority order, filters idle activities by world availability, and supplies deterministic per-state duration ranges.
- `src/villagers/villager.ts` owns the wobbled pill-bodied villager mesh, deterministic spawning, continuous steering, activity execution, quantized transforms and poses, emote delegation, and four-frame hut respawn entrance. Nearby fire is checked every simulation step and preempts the timed activity or rain-reaction state; flee targets remain valid even when a villager stands exactly on the danger point. Villagers abandon an extinguished campfire or an emptied pond immediately. The class also owns effects that require private pose/material access: Lightning's 2.5-second char/blink/tip/ghost zap and Rain's leaf-over-head retreat to the nearest built hut. Death asks `World` to create a persistent visual grave.
- `src/powers/drag.ts` owns the single reusable clay-ring reticle and raycaster. `DragTargeting` unifies pointer dragging with the keyboard reticle path, converts ray hits from the floating island's world transform back into local simulation coordinates, resolves target kinds only at the registry boundary, and maintains the target status announcement. Arrow movement is camera-relative after compensating for the island's world rotation, and changing the armed power preserves the pointer gesture captured before the tray callback.
- `src/powers/power.ts` defines the shared cast context, abstract `Power`, and `PowerManager`. The manager resolves targets at cast time, rejects unavailable/cooling powers, advances active three-beat timelines, and exposes normalized cooldown state to the tray.
- `src/powers/meteor.ts` implements Meteor's 1.2-second anticipation, one-frame impact, and aftermath timeline. Render-bound actors animate and dispose the growing shadow, falling wobbled meteor, and expanding dust ring; impact coordinates shake, pooled debris, persistent crater/scorch mutation, flower cleanup, prop re-grounding, events, nearby fire seeds, and the temporary Task 12 target outcomes for trees, huts, villagers, ponds, and campfires.
- `src/powers/lightning.ts` implements Lightning's 0.9-second curious-cloud anticipation, two-quantized-frame clay-white tube bolt with two forks, crack/flash/shake impact, and target-specific Task 14 outcome shim. Its transient cloud/bolt actors dispose their geometries and materials; the cloud's render-driven animation is kept active at edge casts, and persistent fire, fish, campfire, and villager consequences cross through `World` or the target object.
- `src/powers/rain.ts` implements Rain's arriving five-blob cloud, six-second shower, departure, and half-second nurture ticks. One 120-instance droplet mesh is continuously recycled against `Island.sampleHeight()` and one 24-instance ripple pool shows two-frame impacts; rim casts rejection-sample droplet offsets so rain never lands on empty air beyond the island. The tick repeatedly douses fire and idempotently fills craters, grows fruit, blooms one flower patch, and refreshes villager rain reactions.
- `src/ui/tray.ts` creates the semantic powers toolbar from real buttons, owns shortcut arming and the visual/accessibility enabled state, and exposes the normalized cooldown and availability setters used by the composition root. Each native button sits inside a presentation-only clay shell: the button owns disabled/focus/input semantics while its sibling fill owns cooldown rendering. The fill is quantized to 12 visual steps and DOM writes are guarded; this keeps the claymation cadence while avoiding WKWebView compositor failures caused by combining unavailable state with hundreds of per-cooldown overlay mutations.
- `src/ui/tokens.ts` is the design-token source of truth for the game palette, typography, and timing. Visual modules consume the exported string and numeric constants; the module remains independent of Three.js.
- `src/styles.css` defines the global document, canvas, overlay, splash, focus, reduced-motion rules, irregular clay power controls, cooldown skin, responsive tray layout, and target readout. The UI overlay has an explicit compositor layer, native button appearance is neutralized, focus outlines preserve each clay control's silhouette, and the cooldown uses a clipped solid fill rather than WebKit's artifact-prone animated conic gradients. Its small CSS custom-property set mirrors the matching values from `src/ui/tokens.ts` as required by the implementation plan.
- `index.html` owns the permanent `#app` canvas mount, `#ui` overlay root, loading splash, and no-JavaScript fallback.

## Key patterns

- Visual colors are named semantically in `PALETTE` rather than invented inside feature modules.
- Model animation uses the shared 12fps value in `TIMING`; UI transitions remain smooth and use separate durations.
- The full-screen `#ui` layer ignores pointer input by default, while its direct children opt back in.
- Reduced-motion users receive effectively instant CSS animations and transitions.
- Browser globals stay at the visual composition boundary; pure modules remain safe to exercise in Vitest's Node environment.
- Core simulation modules do not import Three.js. Seeded randomness, noise, event delivery, quantized time, and staged timelines are deterministic or directly unit-testable.
- Visual geometry is made irregular once at construction time, while the clay material supplies reusable surface character at render time. Both effects are procedural and require no asset files.
- `Island.sampleHeight()` is the single terrain-height authority. It combines the undeformed procedural surface with all active crater offsets so walkers and props cannot diverge from visible terrain deformation.
- Island surface colors have two explicit layers: mutable `baseColors` hold grass, dirt fringe, and exposed crater soil; `scorchAmt` accumulates permanent radial burn strength and blends the visible color from that preserved base toward the scorch token. This prevents repeated paint from drifting past its intended color and keeps crater/scorch ordering stable.
- Ambient camera, island, and cloud motion consult the live reduced-motion preference; direct camera input remains enabled.
- Normal rendering caps DPR at 1.25 and uses one 2048px VSM shadow map plus tilt-shift, vignette, 12fps-held grain, and a subtle saturation grade. `?lowfx=1` fixes DPR at 1, disables the shadow map, and retains only the composer render pass.
- Film grain owns an explicit seed uniform rather than using `postprocessing`'s display-rate time uniform. It advances only on the shared 12fps cadence and becomes lower-opacity static grain under reduced motion.
- Repeated small object details use `InstancedMesh`, while stateful trees and huts remain independent object groups. In low-FX mode their child meshes stop casting map shadows and each grounded object receives one transparent blob-shadow disc.
- Particle bursts claim dead slots from a single 400-instance pool, update matrices once per display frame, and use seeded randomness for launch vectors and tumbling. They intentionally stay smooth while villagers and model props remain quantized to 12fps.
- Camera shake removes its prior offset before orbit controls update, then applies the next trauma-squared sample only for rendering. This prevents temporary shake offsets from contaminating OrbitControls' persistent camera state. Reduced motion clears the active offset and trauma immediately; reduced flash uses a 200ms fade-in/out capped at 30% opacity rather than a white strobe.
- Every power is a three-beat `Timeline`: anticipation communicates intent and triggers reactions, impact coordinates spectacle and world mutation, and aftermath leaves consequences. `PowerManager` starts cooldown at cast time and owns timeline/cooldown advancement; visual actors own and dispose their transient Three.js resources.
- Lightning and Rain temporarily dispatch their Task-specific target outcomes at impact while routing all persistent mutation through `World`; Task 16 will replace only those dispatch functions with declarative matrix outcomes. This avoids pulling the full interaction table forward or scattering registry/event logic through transient actor code.
- Rainfall is allocation-bounded during play: droplets and ripples are fixed-size `InstancedMesh` pools whose matrices are rewritten at render time, while cloud geometry is allocated once per cast and disposed after departure. Its consequence ticks are intentionally discrete at 0.5 seconds so area effects remain legible and idempotent.
- Wildfire simulation is Three.js-free and uses only `FIRE_CONFIG`, stable `Flammable` records, fixed-step `dt`, and a dedicated seeded RNG. Burn-out is processed before spread, and the spread-source list is snapshotted so a newly ignited object cannot cascade again in the same tick. `World` applies returned events to visual objects and the shared game bus, keeping simulation, effects, villager alarms, and discovery counts synchronized.
- Fire visuals allocate their clay blobs, smoke, and point lights once per object and only toggle/update those reusable children. At render time `World.updateFireLights()` sorts the lit campfire plus burning objects by camera distance and enables no more than three shadowless fire lights.
- Spatial target resolution is intentionally ordered and linear: living villagers, trees, huts, an intact campfire, ponds, craters, then ground. Dead historical villagers and scattered campfire debris cannot mask visible targets. The registry remains small enough that a spatial index would add complexity without practical benefit.
- Villager decisions use hard narrative triggers before a weighted idle roll: nearby fire overrides every activity, then fresh graves and ruins; only currently available idle actions enter the seeded random selection. This keeps behavior legible without needs, utility scoring, or planning.
- Villager coordinates and timers advance on the fixed 30Hz simulation step, but model positions, facing, waddles, work poses, spawn scale, and emote animation are written only when the 12fps clock changes. This intentional one-frame visual lag is the claymation effect.
- Villager movement uses direct steering plus short-range repulsion from registered solid objects and a hard `WALK_RADIUS` clamp. There is intentionally no navmesh or pathfinding.
- Population is self-healing: once fewer than six villagers remain, `World.repopulate()` waits a seeded 20–30 seconds before spawning one replacement from a built hut. If no villager and no built hut survive, the first ruin becomes a last-resort spawn origin so a replacement can rebuild it; living survivors still rebuild normally instead of spawning from ruins. Dead entries remain in the registry for later grave/discovery history while living-count and targeting checks ignore them.
- Power targeting is modality-independent after arming: pointer rays and keyboard steps both update the same island-local target coordinates, reticle, target-kind lookup, and fire callback. Pointer release away from the island cancels; keyboard Escape cancels and restores tray focus.
- Power cooldowns are normalized values at the tray boundary. The manager registers Meteor, Lightning, and Rain at six, three, and eight seconds respectively; all three controls share the same pointer/keyboard targeting and ARIA cooldown path.

## Classes and database schema

The project-defined classes are `RNG`, `EventBus`, `GameLoop`, `QuantizedClock`, `Timeline`, `FireSystem`, `Island`, `Tree`, `Hut`, `Campfire`, `Pond`, `Grave`, `FlowerPatch`, `World`, `EmoteSprite`, `Villager`, `DragTargeting`, `ParticleBurst`, `Shake`, `Power`, `PowerManager`, `Meteor`, `Lightning`, `Rain`, and the scene-private `HeldNoiseEffect`. `World` stores typed tree, hut, villager, grave, pond, and flower-patch arrays plus one campfire. Fire simulation operates on the in-memory `Flammable` shape (`id`, position, kind, burning/burnt flags, and burn time); graves and ponds are likewise runtime world objects rather than persistent storage. There is no database schema or cross-reload persistence. The scene and composition roots also instantiate Three.js renderer, scene, camera, control, light, geometry, mesh, sprite, instanced-mesh, material, texture, composer, pass, effect, and object-group classes.
